- `zxqec.gpf.simplify_gadgets` / `phase_polynomial`: the layer of Z-type phase gadgets is simplified as a whole. Its phases add up to one multilinear polynomial in the bits of the Z-layer and the outcomes, with coefficients mod 2 (units of pi), which is unique; gadgets that cancel disappear (for pi/4 gadgets this is exactly the triorthogonality condition of the spider-nest identities), Pauli terms move onto the spiders, and the rest is written back as few gadgets as the chosen lifts allow. `reduce_gpf` runs it (by default) whenever the row reduction settles, and reduces again, so rows that only gadgets held become free and detectors that depend on non-Clifford gates are found. Checked on random layers (the map is unchanged for every outcome assignment) and on the thesis's triorthogonal example (all 16 gadgets vanish).
- `zxqec.qprogram.program_from_stim` / `parse_stim`: programs from circuits in Stim's text format, with `T` and `T_DAG`; `DETECTOR` and `OBSERVABLE_INCLUDE` lines are returned separately for comparison, not used to build the program.
- Magic-state cultivation (d = 3) as a third case study: `examples/circuits/cultivation/d3_cultivation.stim` (from Wan, Zhong, Zapirain, Apache-2.0), `examples/Cultivation.ipynb`, `tests/test_cultivation.py` with an independent state-vector simulator (`tests/statevector.py`). The derived detectors equal the 26 the authors declared; the circuit prepares T-dagger|+> in the [[7,1,3]] colour code in every noiseless run, up to a Pauli correction.
- `LogicalAction`: determinism is decided on the matrices (every class of runs equals the first up to a Pauli frame), not on names; runs are named relative to the first, with the smallest correction; projections onto named states, and a logical measurement in a named basis (`measured_basis`), are recognised; more than `limit` distinct parities raises `TooManyRunsError` instead of silently dropping runs. The role `cultivation` checks that the double-check measures the logical qubit in a named basis with an outcome that earlier outcomes predict.
- Termination. The skeleton reduction of `to_gpf` terminates: every bialgebra step lowers the number of Z-spiders that are neither leaves nor stems and touch no input (`skeleton_count`), the argument of Yeh et al., Lemma B.1, with the guards and gadgets added (appendix A of the paper). That number is now the default step bound, replacing `10 (|V| + 1)^2`; exceeding it raises `RuntimeError` as a bug. The alternation of `reduce_gpf` has no termination proof: rounds can change the diagram without deleting a spider. Its cap is now `20 + 2|V|` rounds instead of 20 (one random circuit needed 14). `tests/test_termination.py` checks the count in the real loop (1234 steps on 120 random circuits), and records the rounds the alternation needs (2, 2 and 5 over every reduction of the three case studies, at most 14 on 120 random circuits). Between gadget passes the alternation stops after at most two rounds: `to_gpf` leaves no edge between a Z-spider off the inputs and an X-spider off the outputs (gadgets aside), row reduction keeps it so, and then a second round changes nothing (appendix A, lemma 'two rounds'); with `gadgets=False` the whole alternation takes at most two rounds. The gadget passes can create such edges (100 of 234 simplifications, 5 of 49 changes of variables on the test circuits), so termination of the full loop remains open. `tests/data/alternation_counterexamples.json`: 16 diagrams with such an edge on which the alternation needs three or four rounds, showing the hypothesis is needed.
- `tests/test_sampling.py` with `tests/program_sampling.py`: the derived detectors of the three case studies against sampled runs, from a state-vector simulator that does not use ZX (inputs in random states). Every run satisfies every detector, and the outcome strings span an affine space of dimension (results) - (independent detectors): 4 = 13 - 9 for the `T` injection, 10 = 34 - 24 for the fold-transversal Hadamard, 5 = 31 - 26 for cultivation; a deterministic parity outside the detectors would keep the samples in a smaller space, so this shows that none is missed. The mis-wired fold-transversal Hadamard fills 12 = 34 - 22 dimensions and breaks detectors of the correct circuit. `examples/TrackingOutcomeVariables.ipynb` re-executed (PyZX's rewrite order has changed since it was written) and its text brought in line with the new traces and with the current classification (`c[27]` and `c[31]` are tied, not free).
- `examples/StabilizerCodes.ipynb` and `examples/TransversalGates.ipynb`: demos of the three questions for codes that are not CSS (the `[[5,1,3]]` code, the Steane code after one `S`) and of the role `transversal gate` (two Steane blocks, the `[[4,2,2]]` code). Fixed along the way: a sign that is a sum mod 2 printed its constant unreduced (`c[3] + 8`); the check of a logical gate printed `implements None` for an operation outside the named list, and the summary table showed `None` for it (now `not recognised`, as in the paper).
- The role `transversal gate`: the checks of `logical gate` (one operation, in every run), and that the block is transversal for the code before it: every group of qubits that its multi-qubit gates connect, directly or through a chain of gates, holds at most one qubit of each code block, so a fault cannot spread inside a block. `logical gate` is unchanged, since a logical gate need not be transversal. `qprogram.summary.transversality_violations(circuit, code)` returns where it fails. The transversal `CNOT` of the Steane injection and the transversal `H` of the fold-transversal Hadamard pass; `CNOT(0,1)` twice inside the Steane code (the logical identity) fails, and so does a chain `CNOT(0,7)`, `CNOT(7,1)` through the other block. Tests in `tests/test_codes.py`.
- `zxqec.qprogram.check_detectors`: declared detectors (for example Stim's `DETECTOR` lines, as returned by `program_from_stim`) checked against the ones the tool derives. Each is `deterministic` (a sum of derived detectors; its value 0 or 1 is reported), `not deterministic` (two runs that satisfy every derived detector, with a non-zero reduced diagram, give it different values; the runs are returned), or `unknown` (neither; with non-Clifford gates left in the diagram the derived detectors need not be complete, so such a detector is not called wrong). Derived detectors that are not sums of declared ones are listed too. On cultivation all 26 declared detectors are deterministic; with the gadget layer held fixed, the two that need the T gates are `unknown`; a declared detector with one result left out is `not deterministic`, and the two runs are checked against the original circuit by tensor contraction in `tests/test_declared.py`. `examples/Cultivation.ipynb` shows the call.
- Questions 2 and 3 for any stabilizer code, not only CSS codes. `StabilizerCode.encoder()` builds the ideal encoder of a code that is not CSS from its Choi state, following Yeh, Huang, Kissinger, Li and van de Wetering (*A three-way normal form for stabiliser codes*): `sum_b |b> (x) E|b>` is the stabilizer state with the generators `I (x) g` for the (signed) stabilizers and `X_i (x) Xbar_i`, `Z_i (x) Zbar_i` for the canonical logical operators; its tableau is factored into an inner CSS code and a diagonal Clifford with their formula `G = R N + N^T R^T + R H N^T R^T` (`H G = N`), drawn as `D sum_y (-1)^(rho.y) |yH + t>` with `D` the `S` and `CZ` gates of `G` and outcome-dependent signs in the phases, and its first `k` outputs become the inputs. Their unique normal forms (reduced and isometry AP normal form) are not computed; the logical action does not need them. CSS codes keep the two-layer encoder, which the new construction reproduces. `logical_action`, run classes, verdicts, `stays_in_code`, `summary()` and `compare_programs` now work in such codes. Checked against the definition with numpy alone (`E X_i = Xbar_i E`, `E Z_i = Zbar_i E`, image the signed code space), including codes whose logical operators mix X and Z, and every stage of random programs against `B E_in = E_out L`; the `[[5,1,3]]` code is kept by transversal `HS` and `SH` (named logical gates) and left by `H`, `S` and `T` on one qubit. Fixed: `LogicalAction.parts()` merged a code block that overlaps two groups into the first only, giving overlapping parts (and a crash when naming such a block); the merge is now transitive. Tests in `tests/test_noncss.py` (step 3) and `tests/test_codes.py`.
- `zxqec.qprogram.compare_programs`: two programs (for example a circuit as written and as compiled) compared block by block, a logical equivalence weaker than equality of the circuits. Two blocks agree when the codes before and after them are the same (stabilizer groups, signs ignored, qubits matched by program label or by physical position) and their logical operations agree up to a Pauli frame (every class of runs of one equals a class of the other up to a Pauli, and the other way round); the whole programs are compared the same way, and so are their detectors (number of independent ones, and their span when the results are named alike). A column shows, per block, how many of its results some detector of the whole program mentions in each program, which locates lost checks (results that decide a correction count too). On the case studies: the thesis's mis-wired fold-transversal H differs from the corrected one only in its detectors (24 against 22, both lost in the round with the wrong check); measuring the Steane Z checks first, or using S instead of S-dagger in the correction, is equivalent; leaving out the correction changes the logical action of the injection; wiring the first Z check to the wrong qubit changes the code after the check block. The verdict is three-valued: equivalent, NOT equivalent (something compared differs), or not decided (nothing differs, but something could not be compared, e.g. a code that differs between runs). `tests/programs.steane_t_injection` gained `correction`, `z_checks_first` and `first_z_check` for these variants; `tests/test_compare.py`; `examples/ComparingPrograms.ipynb` walks through the five cases.
- Fixed: `logical_action` named a block's operation even when the block does not map the code before it into the code after it. The composite is reduced up to a non-zero scalar, so `T` on 7 of the 15 qubits of the `[[15,1,3]]` code, which is `0.75 T^dag` (up to a phase) between the ideal encoders and keeps a run in the code with probability 9/16 only, came out as `T^dag`. The new `LogicalAction.stays_in_code()` checks the hypothesis of the paper's proposition on the logical action: it reads the stabilizers of the Choi state of (ideal encoder ; block) and requires every stabilizer of the code after the block to be among them up to sign. A block that fails is reported as leaving the code (`verdict`, `is_deterministic`, and in `summary()` the logical-action column and a failed check), not as a gate. `code_of(..., common_to_all_runs=True)` reads the stabilizers common to all runs instead of raising `OutcomeDependentCodeError`, which the check needs for conditional logical gates such as the Steane injection's correction. No case study changes: every block of the Steane injection and of cultivation (grouped as in the paper) stays in its code. Tests: transversal `T` on `[[15,1,3]]` is logical `T^dag`; `T` on 7 of its qubits leaves the code.
- `examples/Cultivation.ipynb`, Part 4: the reduction drawn step by step, following the thesis (Figs. 4.18-4.23): the whole circuit (370 spiders), the normal form (15 T-type gadgets), the 15 gadgets written in the code's variables (logical, stabilizer and two hidden columns: the 7 T gates read the code's columns, the 7 T-dagger gates the same columns plus the hidden ones, block form) and shown to have the phase polynomial of one T-dagger gadget on the logical variable, with the two T-dependent detectors coming from the sums over the hidden variables, the reduced diagram, the same diagram after a change of summation variables that makes its rows the code's logical X and generators, and the expected diagram, T-dagger|+> fed into the ideal encoder with the Z correction. The last two are identical, spider for spider; every picture equals the circuit, by tensor contraction, in all 32 runs the detectors allow. `tests/test_cultivation.py` checks the same claims.
- Codes that are not CSS. `to_gpf` no longer rejects a diagram with a cycle of an odd number of Hadamard edges: the edges that close such cycles become `CZ`s written as `pi/2` phase gadgets (`y_u y_v = (y_u + y_v - y_u xor y_v)/2`), and every non-Pauli phase is kept on a Z-spider (`cz_gadgets=False` restores the old `ValueError`). `code_of` reads the stabilizers of any stabilizer code: a combination of internal Z-spiders whose flip changes the gadgets' phase by a Pauli `Z` string `d . y` gives the stabilizer `Z^w X^{aM}` with `w . (yA) = d . y` (the `[H_X | H_X G]` form), with its sign including the `i` of each `Y`; if no such `w` exists the string is a logical operator. `StabilizerCode.sx_z` holds the Z parts, and fixed qubits, restriction, blocks and distance work for such codes (fixed qubits can be `+i`/`-i`). The `[[5,1,3]]` code comes out as `XZZXI` and its cyclic shifts, and the Steane code after one `S` as the Steane code conjugated by `S` (it used to come out as `[[7,2,1]]`). A Clifford phase controlled by an outcome, which makes the code itself differ between runs, raises `OutcomeDependentCodeError`. `tests/test_noncss.py` checks every reported stabilizer `P` against the diagram's own matrix, `P E = E` with the sign, in every allowed run, including 40 random multi-stage Clifford programs.
- `code_of`'s gadget test tries one combination per pattern of gadget parities instead of every combination of internal spiders, refuses beyond 14 independent parities instead of falling back to a coarser rule, and marks a code non-Clifford only when some flip changes the phase by more than a Pauli (a transversal `S` no longer counts). `summary()` reports a block whose code or logical action cannot be computed as a problem in its row instead of raising.
- `reduce_gpf`, for Clifford phases: a Z-spider that only gadgets hold and whose phases are Clifford in its bit is summed out in closed form (a detector, a parity check on other bits, or one gadget `-a F`, i.e. a local complementation); Z-rows read by gadgets that depend on other rows are reduced by a change of variables that the gadgets follow; and a parity check left on such rows is solved for one of its bits. With these, detectors are exact (zero branch if and only if a reported detector is violated) on random Clifford circuits with `S` gates and mid-circuit measurements, which they were not before (an `S` just before a measurement hid detectors).
- Fixed: `to_gpf` could make a degree-one phase spider the leaf of an X-spider that sits on a boundary wire, or a second leaf on a gadget hub; neither is a phase gadget (the leaf's bit is summed over), and the code reading misread it. Such a spider now gets its own gadget, and `is_gpf` rejects both shapes.
- Fixed: the symplectic Gram-Schmidt of `_canonical_logicals`, used for codes without pure-`X` logical operators, removed elements from a list of numpy arrays by value and crashed.
- Fixed (in the gadget step added above, before release): moving Pauli terms onto spiders could rewrite the gadget layer into a much larger one (15 leaves became 35 on the walkthrough's T injection with an `S` correction), after which `logical_action` hit `TooManyRunsError`. The layer is never made larger now; the outcome part of a leaf phase is moved to the spiders the gadget reads without touching the rest of the layer.
- `zxqec.codes`: the three questions of the paper. `code_of` reads the stabilizer code a Clifford isometry encodes into off its generalized parity form (Kissinger's reading, with stabilizer signs that are functions of measurement outcomes); gadgets are handled exactly by a phase-polynomial test (`zxqec.codes.phasepoly`) that decides which X-strings survive a layer of non-Clifford phases, so an encoded magic state is reported as a code holding a non-Clifford state, and a transversal conditional `S` keeps the Steane code. `StabilizerCode` gives minimum-weight generators (measured checks show their own outcome as sign), canonical logical operators that depend only on the stabilizer group, fixed helper qubits, independent blocks, brute-force distance, comparison by program qubit or by physical position, `contains`, and the ideal `encoder()`. `logical_action` computes what a block does between the ideal encoder of the code before it and the ideal decoder of the code after it, and names each class of runs (factor by factor over code blocks, up to a Pauli); `LogicalAction.verdict` says whether the block is deterministic, deterministic up to a Pauli frame, or outcome-dependent, covering every run through the finitely many values of the parities left in the reduced diagram.
- `QProgram.prefix_graph`, `code_at`, `logical_action`, `group_stages`, and `summary(level)`: the level-up table, one row per block with its codes, logical action, measurements and the checks implied by the block's `role` (encoder, syndrome extraction, logical gate, logical measurement, state preparation, correction, injection, ...). Blocks and `level_up` / `merge_blocks` take a `role`.
- `zxqec.qprogram.ParityControlled`: a correction applied when the XOR of some measurement outcomes is 1, kept symbolic in the diagram (Paulis as one spider with phase `pi p`; rotations as a half-angle spider plus a gadget whose hub carries `p`).
- `examples/TInjectionWalkthrough.ipynb` with `examples/teaching_figures.py`; `tests/programs.py` (the Steane T-injection program) and `tests/test_codes.py`, including a regression test that the level-up table flags the mis-wired check of the thesis's fold-transversal H circuit.
- Repository skeleton: package layout (`qprogram`, `spacetime`, `tableau`, `topological`, `gui`), packaging, tests.
- `zxqec.qprogram.QProgram`: programs as sequences of circuit stages and permutations on a hardware layout; master space-time graph with `stage` / `logical_qubit` vertex data on top of `pyzx.spacetime`; stage and block sub-diagrams; measured qubits reborn by `Reset`; per-stage renaming of PyZX's generated symbolic variables.
- `zxqec.qprogram.LevelManager` / `Block`: level-up of consecutive stages and merging of blocks separated by stages on disjoint qubits (port of the thesis `GateLevelManager`).
- `zxqec.spacetime.Topology` (port of the thesis class, with `grid` / `line` constructors) and `induced_subdiagram`, which cuts any set of spiders out of a program graph as a valid ZX-diagram, keeping idle wires and attributing every edge to its later endpoint.
- `zxqec.gpf`: reduction to generalized parity form with phases. `to_gpf` (thesis Algorithm 2) keeps Pauli/Boolean phases on the spiders and uses PyZX's Boolean bialgebra rule; non-Pauli phases become stem+leaf gadgets; Hadamard edges are removed by recolouring and recorded on boundary wires. `reduce_gpf` (Algorithm 3) does the split-matrix row reduction of both layers over GF(2) with the phases carried along -- only free rows are ever sources -- and returns the detectors (Boolean parities of measurement outcomes that must vanish) and whether the diagram is zero. `isolate_paulis=True` gives the thesis's phase-free skeleton.
- Fixed: `measurement_report` reported a Boolean phase sitting on a *gadget hub* as part of the Pauli frame. Such a phase negates the gadget's phase instead (a `pi/4` leaf acts as `-pi/4`, i.e. `T` becomes `T-dagger`), so following `frame_values` gave the wrong correction -- on the Steane T injection it claimed a `Z` where a conditional `S` is needed. The report now separates the two: `frame` and `frame_values` carry only Pauli corrections, and `gadget_signs` / `gadget_sign_values` carry the conditional rotations. (Reported by an external review of the code.)
- `to_gpf` removes identity spiders and fuses same-colour neighbours before pushing Hadamards to the boundary. Two consequences: a diagram whose only odd-Hadamard cycle runs through an identity is now accepted rather than rejected, and two gadgets sharing a parity are fused, which recovers a detector the reduction used to miss (`pi/4` and `3pi/4` leaves hiding a `pi` phase).
- `push_hadamards_to_boundary` now chooses each component's colouring so that boundary Hadamards land on inputs where possible, so a reduced diagram reads "Hadamard first, then the outcome-dependent Paulis" -- the order in which a decoder applies corrections. `frame` is reported from input to output, and `frame_values` documents that it is read in the input frame.
- `to_gpf` checks its post-conditions before returning, and `is_gpf` now also checks (G3)/(G4) -- non-Pauli phases only on gadget leaves. `reduce_gpf` raises instead of stopping silently when the Z/X alternation does not settle within `max_rounds`, and `phase_mod2` rejects a non-Pauli constant rather than truncating it.
- `tests/test_gpf_random.py` checks the detectors themselves: every violated constraint must give a zero branch and every allowed branch must be non-zero (completeness asserted for Pauli-only diagrams).
- Fixed: the zero verdict of `reduce_gpf` looked for the constant 1 among the constraints, which misses an inconsistent system such as `{c, c + 1}` where every branch vanishes. It now solves the constraints over GF(2); the new `zxqec.gpf.constraint_system`, `unsatisfiable` and `detector_rank` expose that system, the verdict and the number of independent detectors.
- Fixed: an isolated Z--X pair was dropped as a unit scalar regardless of its phases, but `<X(3pi/2)|Z(pi/2)>` is exactly zero. Such a pair is now dropped only when at least one phase is Pauli. (Reported by an external review of the draft; PyZX's own scalar bookkeeping already caught the case that reaches it through `to_gpf`.)
- Fixed: `equal_for_all_assignments` reported a false mismatch whenever a branch of the protocol is the zero map, because `pyzx.compare_tensors` normalises by the scalar. The new `zxqec.gpf.same_map` compares two diagrams with the scalar kept and treats two zero maps as equal; the verification helper uses it.
- Fixed: a spider with a non-Pauli phase sitting directly on a boundary wire was treated as a gadget leaf, after which the boundary padding put two same-coloured spiders in a row and the result was not in GPF shape (the map was still correct). Found by random differential testing against the thesis implementation; `tests/test_gpf_random.py` now checks `to_gpf` and `reduce_gpf` on random diagrams with Pauli, Boolean and non-Pauli phases.
- `zxqec.gpf.measurement_report` and `QProgram.measurement_report(level, index, context=True)`: the detectors and the Pauli frame turned into a per-measurement table -- each outcome is part of the frame (the map depends on it), fixed by a detector, tied to other outcomes, or gauge -- plus the dimensions of the outcome space (`forced + steering + gauge`), `violated(outcomes)` and `frame_values(outcomes)` for checking a run, and `internal_detectors()` for the ones a block can check on its own. A block's measurements are classified in the context of the whole program by default (`context=False` analyses the block alone, where nothing is deterministic).
- `QProgram.add_circuit(..., physical=True)` for stages written in the physical frame (qubit indices are wire positions, relabelled through the current mapping) and `QProgram.quantum_graph()` (master graph without the classical-bit wires); `stage_graph` / `block_graph` take `classical=False` for the same on a sub-diagram.
- `examples/SteaneTInjection.ipynb`: the second case study, a `T` gate injected into the Steane code. A derived-and-checked encoder with its transversal gates (`H^7` is logical H, `S^7` is logical S-dagger, seven CNOTs are a logical CNOT), the physical protocol as a 20-qubit program, level-up into logical operations with the measurement report per block, and the logical operator it implements per branch. The conditional-`S` correction is a conditional Clifford, which the symbolic framework cannot carry, so the branches are checked separately -- the smallest realistic motivation for feedforward support.

The example notebooks use this for the 2D views; the matplotlib views remain for scripts and static figures and gained a `classical` option (classical-bit wires are no longer drawn by default).
